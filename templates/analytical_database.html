A closer look at the analytical database project I developed for VanWonen.

The initial goal was to build a set of databases to store structured and standardized information about real estate development projects and their business cases. By organizing this information properly, we could connect it to dashboards and make it much easier to track project progress, compare different projects and business cases, and make better informed decisions without spending so much time gathering and processing information.

Over time, we applied the same approach to other types of information, such as market research, potential acquisitions, customer and partner information, and more.

But regardless of the type of data, I think the biggest challenge is always the same: figuring out how to structure it properly while keeping in mind what people actually need from it.

For me, that's the most interesting part of this kind of work. It's not just about building databases or moving data around. It's about understanding how people work, what they're trying to achieve, and how we can make their jobs easier and more efficient, while ultimately helping the company make better decisions and achieve better business results.

To structure the data and make it useful for different purposes, I used Python to connect to various sources of information, including Microsoft applications, real estate software, APIs, customer information, public data and statistics, etc.

I then used Python to clean, transform and organize the data according to what the end users needed, before loading it into SQL databases. From there, the data could be connected to visualization tools like Tableau, making it easier to explore, analyse and use in everyday decision making or analysis.
